You are probably paying a flat monthly fee for a Claude or ChatGPT subscription and using a fraction
of it. ApexOne lets you put the rest to work: connect your account, and when someone else's request
is served by your idle quota, you take a share of what they paid.

<Aside type="caution" title="What we commit to — and what we don't">
	We commit to the **revenue share** (currently 50%) and to how you get paid. We do **not** commit to
	how much you will earn: that depends on real demand on the platform, and the platform is early with
	demand still building. Early earnings may be quite limited. Any figure you calculate from the
	formula below is an estimate, not a promise.
</Aside>

## We can't see your chats

This is the first thing most people want to know, so here it is before anything else.

What you hand over is a **key that does exactly one thing**: run other people's requests against
your quota. Reading your chat history takes a different key, and we never asked for it.

So this isn't us promising not to look — **we simply don't hold that key.** A promise asks you to
trust us. "We never requested that permission" is something you can check for yourself, on the
authorization screen, before you approve anything.

<Aside type="tip" title="In one line">
	✓ What it can do: run other people's requests on your quota<br />
	✗ What it cannot do: read your chats, your profile, your files
</Aside>

### What connecting does mean

Being clear about what can't happen also means being clear about what does:

- **Your quota gets used.** That is the whole point — other people's requests draw on what you
  didn't use, and you take a share. It shows up in your vendor account as usage.
- **You can set a daily cap** on every account; it stops taking requests once the cap is reached and
  resumes the next day, so your quota is never drained without a limit you chose.
- **What does not happen** is anyone reading, exporting, or training on what **you** did in that
  account.

### Want to verify?

Exactly one permission is requested when you authorize:

| Permission requested | What it allows |
| --- | --- |
| `user:inference` | Send inference requests against your quota. Nothing else. |

These are permissions that exist in Anthropic's OAuth and that we deliberately **do not** request:

| Permission we do NOT request | What it would have allowed |
| --- | --- |
| `user:sessions:claude_code` | Reading your Claude Code sessions and history |
| `user:profile` | Reading your account profile |
| `user:file_upload` | Uploading files as you |
| `user:mcp_servers` | Reaching your connected MCP servers |
| `org:create_api_key` | Minting API keys on your organization |

You can check both tables in two places: the authorization screen before you approve, and the
[public source](https://github.com/FiiLabs/sub2api).

<Aside type="note" title="ChatGPT accounts">
	A ChatGPT connection requests identity information and a refresh token, which means we do see the
	email address the account belongs to (it is shown on your own account list). Reading your chats is
	not requested either.
</Aside>

## How it works

<Steps>

1. **Connect your subscription.** Authorize an account through OAuth, or supply a compatible API
   endpoint. Either way you can take it offline or disconnect it at any time.

2. **Your idle quota gets used.** The platform routes requests to your account, drawing only on
   quota you have not used yourself.

3. **You take a share of the revenue.** For every fee the platform charges the user, you keep 50%.

</Steps>

## Walkthrough: from zero to earning

This is the whole flow, click by click. It takes a few minutes; the waiting afterwards takes longer
than the doing.

<TutorialVideo
	title="ApexOne tutorial: from sign-up to sharing a subscription"
	description="Prefer to watch first? The same walkthrough on screen, about 2.5 minutes."
/>

<Steps>

1. **Sign in and open the sharing console.**

   Go to the [ApexOne Dashboard](https://apex1.us) and sign in. In the left sidebar, find the
   **Earn by Sharing** section and click **Share Subscription**.

   If you don't see that section, sharing is not enabled on this site — nothing you can fix from
   your side; contact the operator.

2. **Read and accept the supplier agreement.**

   This is a one-time gate: nothing can be connected before it's accepted. It's worth actually
   reading the part about data handling — your obligations differ depending on which connection
   method you pick in the next step.

   Tick the box and click **Accept and continue**.

3. **Pick the platform, then connect your subscription.**

   Choose whether you're sharing a **Claude** or a **ChatGPT** subscription, then connect. Claude
   offers two methods (see [the comparison below](#two-ways-to-connect) — if you're not sure, take
   OAuth); ChatGPT connects via OAuth only.

   **OAuth — recommended (both Claude and ChatGPT).** Click **Connect my subscription**. A new tab
   opens the vendor's authorization page. Approve it, copy the code it hands you, paste it back into
   the form, and click **Finish connecting**. The link is valid for about 15 minutes; if it expires,
   just start over.

   **API relay (Claude only).** Fill in an Anthropic-compatible base URL (`https://…`, no path
   suffix — the platform appends `/v1/messages` itself) and its API key. On submit the platform
   sends one minimal real request to check the endpoint answers, so a wrong key fails immediately
   rather than silently.

4. **Wait out the review period.**

   Your account now shows **In review**. The platform probes it periodically; once it has passed
   consecutive probes and the minimum observation window has elapsed, it joins the pool and starts
   taking traffic.

   **You earn nothing during this period. That is expected, not a fault.**

5. **Bind a payout address — you can do this while waiting.**

   In the withdrawal section, paste your wallet address for the payout chain. Copy it from your
   wallet; do not type it by hand.

6. **Watch it work.**

   Once the account is serving, **Total earned** starts moving and each request shows up in the
   earnings ledger. Earnings sit in a hold window first, then become withdrawable.

</Steps>

<Aside type="tip" title="If nothing is happening after review passes">
	An account that is in the pool but earning nothing simply has no traffic routed to it yet. That's
	a demand-side reality — there is nothing to fix in your configuration. Check the account state
	first: **In review** and **Serving** mean very different things.
</Aside>

## Two ways to connect

<CardGrid>
	<Card title="OAuth (recommended)" icon="approve-check-circle">
		Authorize your Claude or ChatGPT account and paste the code back. Requests reach the vendor
		**through our TEE gateway** — they never touch any device of yours, so you never handle user
		data.
	</Card>
	<Card title="API relay (Claude only)" icon="setting">
		Supply an Anthropic-compatible `/v1/messages` endpoint and its key. Requests are forwarded to
		**your** server, which means user prompts pass through infrastructure you operate — see the
		obligations below. Site operators can disable this route.
	</Card>
</CardGrid>

<Aside type="note" title="If you connect a relay endpoint">
	Relayed requests carry other people's prompt content to a server you control. You are responsible
	for handling that data properly; the supplier agreement spells out the obligation. If you would
	rather not take that on, use the OAuth route — it never routes user content through your machines.
</Aside>

## The review period

A newly connected account does **not** start serving immediately. It enters a review period where
the platform probes it periodically and records the result. The account only joins the pool once it
has passed consecutive probes **and** the minimum observation window has elapsed.

This exists to protect both sides: you don't want your first impression of the platform to be a
stream of failures from a misconfigured endpoint, and consumers shouldn't be routed to an account
that cannot serve them.

<Aside type="tip" title="Watch the probe result, not the clock">
	If probing fails, the dashboard shows why. The most common cause is an expired authorization —
	reconnecting the account fixes it. A failed probe resets the consecutive-success counter, so a
	flaky endpoint will sit in review indefinitely rather than joining the pool and failing users.
</Aside>

## How the revenue share is calculated

```
Your earnings = what the user paid for that usage × revenue share (50%)
```

The base is **what the platform charges the user**, not the vendor's official list price. This
distinction matters, because ApexOne bills consumers at a fraction of official list pricing, and
that fraction depends on the platform — Claude usage is billed at 14% of list, ChatGPT usage at 20%:

| Per $1 of official list-price usage | Claude | ChatGPT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The user is charged | $0.14 | $0.20 |
| **You receive (50%)** | **$0.07** | **$0.10** |
| The platform keeps (50%) | $0.07 | $0.10 |

The revenue share is **50% on both platforms**; what differs is the consumer's billing rate, which
sets the base your share applies to.

### What that looks like on a real task

The table above is per dollar of list price, which is hard to picture. Here is the same maths on
three coding tasks of different sizes, priced with Claude Fable 5's published rates (input $10 /
MTok, output $50 / MTok, cache reads $1 / MTok). Agentic coding is cache-heavy, so cache reads
dominate the token count:

| Task | Cache reads | Fresh input | Output | List price | User pays | **You receive** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fix a small bug | 60K | 2K | 3K | $0.23 | $0.032 | **$0.016** |
| Build a feature | 250K | 6K | 15K | $1.06 | $0.148 | **$0.074** |
| Refactor a module | 900K | 20K | 50K | $3.60 | $0.504 | **$0.252** |

Twenty "build a feature" tasks in a day would put roughly **$1.48** in your wallet. Multiply or
divide as your account's actual traffic dictates.

<Aside type="caution" title="This is arithmetic, not a forecast">
	Every number above follows from published list prices and the rates on this page — you can redo
	the calculation yourself. What it does **not** tell you is how many tasks will be routed to your
	account, and that is the whole variable. An account in the pool with no demand routed to it earns
	nothing, however the table looks. We commit to the rates, not to a volume.
</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="Why the share is a percentage of the charge, not of list price">
	Quoting "50% of list price" would be a much bigger number and a false one. Everything downstream —
	your ledger, your withdrawals, our reconciliation — is denominated in what was actually collected,
	so that is what the share applies to.
</Aside>

## What you can and cannot see

You see: which of your accounts served how many requests, what each one earned, and every ledger
entry behind those numbers.

You do **not** see: who the consumer was, or what they asked. On the OAuth route the content never
reaches you in the first place; in the ledger, consumer identity is stripped server-side before the
data leaves the API.

## Taking an account back

Two different actions, and the difference matters:

- **Take offline** — stops new requests and enters a drain window. Requests already streaming finish
  normally, and you can cancel while the window is open. The credential stays with the platform.
- **Disconnect** — stops new requests *and* permanently deletes the authorization credential we hold.
  This cannot be undone, and reconnecting means going through the review period again.

Earnings already accrued are unaffected by either action, and remain withdrawable.

<Aside type="note" title="Disconnecting doesn't revoke the grant upstream">
	Deleting our copy of the credential stops ApexOne from using the account. To revoke the
	authorization on the vendor's side as well, remove it in your Claude or ChatGPT (OpenAI) account
	settings.
</Aside>
